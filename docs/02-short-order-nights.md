# Chapter 2: Short-order nights (the ADHD-friendly backbone)

A **short-order night** is a planned night where dinner requires almost no decision, almost no effort, and almost no dishes. It's the load-bearing wall of this whole system: every weekly plan in this manual reserves **two or more nights** for them.

> This is an accessibility and energy-management technique, not a willpower medal. If your evenings are low-capacity, the plan should say so out loud. That's what the template does.

## Why "planned lazy" beats "motivated"

Most meal plans fail for one reason: they assume a version of you that exists on Sunday but not on Wednesday at 6:15 p.m. Short-order nights are deliberate slack — they absorb the hard days so the rest of the plan survives.

The three rules of a short-order night:

1. **No new decisions.** The meal and its steps are written down in advance.
2. **Five steps max, ten minutes max of active work.** If it needs chopping or timing, it doesn't belong here.
3. **No backup needed.** Cereal, leftovers, or the freezer stash all count as success.

## The fill-in template

Copy this block into any AI chat and fill in your own lines, or print it ([Chapter 7](07-printable-planner.md)) and fill it in by hand. This is **Template T-1** in the [prompt library](06-prompt-library.md).

```
Let's design 2 short-order nights for my week.

My constraints:
- Active cooking time: 10 minutes max
- Steps: 5 steps max, written out fully
- Equipment available: [e.g., microwave, toaster oven, stovetop]
- Household: [e.g., 2 adults, 1 kid who eats plain pasta]
- Go-to shortcuts I already trust: [e.g., frozen dumplings,
  canned tomato soup, boxed mac]
- Stretch goals only if easy: [e.g., frozen veg microwaved in a bag]

For each short-order night, give me a card in this exact format:

NIGHT: ___
MEAL: ___
STEPS: numbered 1–5 max, each one sentence
DIRTY DISHES: list (aim for 2 or fewer)
KID VARIANT (if any): one sentence
BACKUP: what I eat if I can't even do this
GROCERY STAPLES USED: so I can check I'm stocked

No nutrition or health advice. Logistics only.
Keep the tone practical.
```

## A worked example of the output

Here's the kind of card you should expect back (yours will differ):

**NIGHT:** Wednesday

**MEAL:** Dumpling bowl

**STEPS:**
1. Put frozen dumplings in a microwave-safe bowl.
2. Microwave 3 minutes, stir, microwave 2 more minutes.
3. Microwave a bag of frozen broccoli per the bag instructions (optional).
4. Splash of soy sauce over the bowl.
5. Eat directly from the bowl. Bowl counts as self-cleaning if you must.

**DIRTY DISHES:** 1 bowl, 1 fork.

**KID VARIANT:** Reserve 4 dumplings before anything seasoned touches them.

**BACKUP:** Cereal, or the pasta-and-cheddar kit in the pantry.

**GROCERY STAPLES USED:** Frozen dumplings, frozen broccoli, soy sauce.

## Planning rule for the week

When you do your Sunday (or whenever) planning session, use this ordering:

1. Pick your **2 short-order nights first** — slot them on your week's two most chaotic days (school nights, shift changes, the day that always goes sideways).
2. Then fill the other 5 with normal pantry-first meals ([Chapter 1](01-pantry-first-prompts.md)).
3. Keep the freezer shelf honest: short-order nights run on a **stash**, so add stash items to the grocery list whenever the shelf is short. The system breaks the week the stash is empty.

## Re-running the template

You don't need to ask the AI every week. Once the cards are good, they're done — reuse them for months. The AI's job was to write the cards the first time and remix them when you get bored:

```
Refresh my short-order night cards. Same constraints as before,
but give me 2 new cards that don't use dumplings or mac.
```

## What "ADHD-friendly" means here, concretely

- **Single location.** Each card says exactly which appliance and bowl you need. No mid-recipe foraging.
- **Timer-friendly steps.** Every step is either "press start" or "watch this one thing."
- **Forgiveness built in.** Every card ends with a BACKUP line so falling short of the meal doesn't cascade into ordering out *and* feeling bad. The backup is part of the plan, not a failure of it.
